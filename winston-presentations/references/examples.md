# Before / after examples

## Opening
**Before:** "Hello everyone, thank you for coming. Today I will talk about my internship. First I will present the company, then the tools..."
**After:** "In 10 minutes you will know how we cut the report generation time from 40 to 5 minutes, and how you could do the same."
*Why:* a concrete promise gives a reason to listen; an agenda gives none.

## A dense slide
**Before:** Title "Architecture", six bullets about services, databases, queues, each with a sentence.
**After:** A single diagram with three boxes and arrows. Text: "One request, three services." The details go in the speaker notes.
*Why:* people cannot read six bullets and listen simultaneously.

## Naming the idea
**Before:** "We used an approach that is a bit like caching but different."
**After:** "We call it *write-once cache*. Not a normal cache: it never invalidates, it only appends."
*Why:* a name plus a fence is easier to remember and to cite.

## Verbal punctuation
"That was the problem. Now, the second part: what we tried. And at the end, what it gave us."

## Ending
**Before:** "Thank you for your attention. Any questions?"
**After:** Last slide: "What you now have: a method that cuts report time by 8x, and the checklist to apply it." Last words: a sincere salute to the audience.
