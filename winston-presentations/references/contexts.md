# Context variants

Pick the closest one. Items marked *(adaptation)* are extensions of Winston's principles to a context he did not cover directly; present them as suggestions.

## Thesis, internship report, or project defense (soutenance)
- Start with the promise, then place the problem in a bigger context (many defenses fail by skipping context).
- Structure: promise, context and problem, approach, what was done (the steps), results, **contributions**.
- Rehearse in front of someone who does not know the subject.
- *(adaptation)* Follow the institution's imposed format (acknowledgments, timing, a final questions slide) and keep the contributions slide just before it.

## Job talk or interview presentation
- In the first ~5 minutes: the **vision** and proof that you **did something**.
- Structure: promise, vision, steps, results, **contributions** (mirroring the steps).
- Focus on what you personally did and what the audience gets from it.

## Class presentation (exposé)
- Promise, one salient idea, a story or example, then the idea cycled again.
- Keep slides sparse; prefer a diagram or prop.
- *(adaptation)* Time per slide is short: plan roughly one slide per minute at most, and fewer for hard ideas.

## Teaching or lecture
- Prefer the **board** over slides for hard material; use slides to expose, not to teach.
- Use the question-and-wait technique several times.
- Cycle each key concept across the session.

## Startup pitch *(adaptation)*
- Open with a promise phrased as the audience's benefit or the problem solved.
- One salient idea (the product's single memorable claim) with a name and a fence against the closest alternative.
- End with what you have achieved and what you are asking for, not a "thank you".

## Technical conference talk
- Promise, problem, the named idea with a fence, evidence, contributions.
- One symbol (diagram) that carries the core idea.
