# Winston's principles (paraphrased)

Why each rule exists, so Claude can adapt it instead of applying it blindly.

## Skill = knowledge + practice + a little talent
Communication quality depends mostly on what you know and how much you practice. Talent matters least. So the skill's job is to supply structure and a practice routine, not to flatter.

## Opening
- Start with an empowerment promise, not a joke. The audience is not yet listening in the first moments.
- Give the audience a reason to stay: something they will know or be able to do afterwards.

## Four techniques
1. **Cycle**: about a fifth of the audience is mentally away at any time. Repeating the idea three times, in different words, catches almost everyone.
2. **Fence**: show what the idea is not, next to the nearest similar idea. Naming the idea makes it easier to remember and to cite.
3. **Verbal punctuation**: signal transitions out loud, like stops on a bus route, so people can get back on.
4. **Question and wait**: a real question, followed by silence of several seconds. Not too easy, not too hard.

## Time, place, room
- Mid-morning is a good time. Avoid right after lunch.
- Lighting: keep it bright; dim rooms make people sleepy.
- Room: not too big, more than half full. Visit it beforehand.
- Nerves: picture an indifferent audience; it cannot be worse than what you imagine.

## Tools
- **Board**: best for teaching. Your writing speed matches the speed people can follow, and your hands have something to do.
- **Props**: people remember what they can see and touch more than what is on a slide.
- **Slides**: good for exposing ideas, poor for teaching. People have one language processor and cannot read and listen at once. Avoid reading slides, small fonts, walls of words, logos, laser pointers. Leave space.

## Informing
Show passion. Tell stories. The goal is to inspire as well as inform.

## Persuading (job talk, thesis defense)
- Put the problem in a larger context so listeners see why it matters.
- Within the first minutes, show the **vision** (an important problem and a new approach) and that you have **done something** (the steps).
- End with the **contributions**, which mirror the steps.
- Rehearse before an audience that does not know the topic.

## Being memorable: the 5 S's
**Symbol**, **Slogan**, **Surprise**, **Salient** idea (it stands out; one only), **Story**.

## Ending
- Last slide: contributions. Collaborators go on the first slide.
- Do not end with a bare "thank you" or "questions?".
- A joke or a sincere salute to the audience is a better final note.
